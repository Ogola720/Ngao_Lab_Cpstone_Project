Context

The Problem

Kenyan microfinance institutions and SACCOs face significant financial risk from loan defaults in the agricultural sector.

  • Manual credit assessments are slow, inconsistent, and prone to human bias
  • Agricultural lending has inherently higher volatility due to seasonal, weather, and market risks
  • ~21.8% of loans default, creating significant financial losses for SACCOs
  • Smallholder farmers lack formal credit histories, making traditional scoring models ineffective

Methodology

Handling Class Imbalance

With ~78% repaid vs ~22% defaulted, the model risks ignoring the minority class. We employed three complementary strategies:

1. SMOTE Oversampling
Synthetic Minority Over-sampling Technique generates synthetic default examples to balance training data distribution.
2. Cost-Sensitive Weighting
scale_pos_weight in XGBoost penalizes misclassifying defaults more heavily than repaid loans.
3. Threshold Optimization
Shifting classification threshold from 0.5 → ~0.20–0.35 to maximize default recall without excessive false positives.

Insights

Key Findings & Takeaways

01
Top Risk Drivers
Repayment delays (mean_repay_delay, max_repay_delay), late_repayment_ratio, and high loan intensity are the strongest predictors.
02
Threshold Tuning > SMOTE
Adjusting classification threshold (0.5 → ~0.25) was more effective than SMOTE alone for catching actual defaults.
03
XGBoost > Neural Networks
Tuned XGBoost consistently outperformed both ShallowMLP and DeepTabNet on this tabular dataset — gradient boosting remains king for structured data.
04
Feature Engineering Matters
Engineered behavioral features (repayment delay ratios, on-time rates) had far higher predictive power than raw demographic features alone.
